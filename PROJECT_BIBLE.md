Project Bible

This document is the highest design authority of the TrustLayer project.

If implementation conflicts with this document, the implementation must be changed—not the philosophy.

---

1. Mission

Reputation belongs to people, not platforms.

People spend years building trust through honest behaviour.

Today, that trust is owned by individual platforms.

TrustLayer exists to return reputation ownership to the people who earn it.

---

2. Vision

Build a decentralized reputation infrastructure where reputation becomes:

* user-owned
* portable
* verifiable
* tamper-resistant
* reusable across different marketplaces

TrustLayer is not another marketplace.

TrustLayer is a reputation infrastructure that existing marketplaces can reference.

---

3. Core Philosophy

People create reputation through their behaviour.

Marketplaces contribute reputation events, but never own user reputation.

The system records history.

The market interprets history.

The Three Design Axioms

TrustLayer is built on three design axioms.

These axioms define the foundation of the project and should remain stable even if technologies, frameworks, blockchains, or implementation details change.

---

Axiom 1 — Ownership

Reputation belongs to people, not platforms.

Reputation is earned by individuals through their behaviour.

Platforms may record, contribute, or reference reputation events, but they must not own user reputation.

TrustLayer exists to move reputation ownership from platforms back to the people who earned it.

---

Axiom 2 — History

History is the asset. Reputation is its interpretation.

The true long-term asset is not a reputation score.

The true asset is the user’s verifiable historical behaviour.

Reputation scores, badges, rankings, and summaries are interpretations of that history.

Algorithms may evolve.

History remains.

---

Axiom 3 — Evidence

Blockchain stores evidence, not reputation.

Blockchain does not create trust.

Blockchain does not judge people.

Blockchain preserves tamper-resistant evidence of historical events.

Evidence forms history.

History supports reputation.

Reputation is interpreted by users, marketplaces, and applications.

---

Summary

Ownership answers:

Who does reputation belong to?

History answers:

What is the real asset?

Evidence answers:

What should blockchain preserve?

Together, these axioms define TrustLayer.

People own reputation.

History creates reputation.

Blockchain protects evidence.

5. Design Goals

TrustLayer should provide:

* Portable reputation
* Immutable reputation history
* Cross-platform reputation
* Verifiable reputation events
* Open APIs
* User ownership

TrustLayer should NOT become:

* A marketplace
* A payment platform
* A centralized reputation authority
* A universal social credit system

---

6. Reputation Model

TrustLayer stores events, not opinions.

Examples of reputation events:

* Trade completed
* Buyer confirmed
* Seller confirmed
* Dispute submitted
* Dispute resolved
* No-show
* False complaint
* Identity verified

These events become permanent historical records.

Reputation is calculated from history.

History itself is never replaced.

---

7. The Role of Blockchain

Blockchain exists to provide:

* immutability
* transparency
* auditability
* ownership

Blockchain is NOT responsible for:

* determining truth
* deciding disputes
* creating trust

It only protects historical records.

---

8. The Role of Users

Users own their reputation.

Users choose to build their reputation.

Users decide whether to expose their reputation.

Users benefit from long-term honest behaviour.

---

9. The Role of Marketplaces

Marketplaces remain responsible for:

* listings
* messaging
* payments
* logistics

TrustLayer only provides reputation services.

Marketplaces may contribute reputation events.

Marketplaces may query reputation through APIs.

---

10. MVP Definition

Phase 1 focuses ONLY on Trading Reputation.

Features:

* User Identity
* Reputation Profile
* Trade Event
* Reputation Event Ledger
* Reputation Viewer

No payment escrow.

No complex arbitration.

No AI judgement.
