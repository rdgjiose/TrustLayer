1. Purpose

This document describes how real users interact with TrustLayer during peer-to-peer trading.

TrustLayer is not inserted at only one moment.

It supports the full trading lifecycle:

Before Trade

↓

During Trade

↓

After Trade

⸻

2. Core Principle

TrustLayer does not guarantee a trade.

TrustLayer does not judge truth.

TrustLayer records verifiable behaviour across the trading lifecycle.

⸻

3. Buyer Journey

Step 1 — Discover Item

The buyer finds an item on an external marketplace.

Examples:

* Facebook Marketplace
* TradeMe
* XHS
* WeChat group

Step 2 — Check Seller Reputation

The item description may include a TrustLayer reputation link.

The buyer opens the link and checks:

* completed trades
* account age
* dispute history
* confirmation rate
* reputation confidence

TrustLayer helps the buyer decide whether to continue.

Step 3 — Contact Seller

The buyer contacts the seller through the original marketplace.

TrustLayer does not replace marketplace messaging.

Step 4 — Create or Join Trade

Either buyer or seller creates a TrustLayer trade link.

Both parties join the trade.

Step 5 — Meet and Complete Trade

The buyer inspects the item.

Payment happens outside TrustLayer.

Examples:

* cash
* bank transfer
* other agreed payment method

TrustLayer does not process payment.

Step 6 — Confirm Trade

After the exchange, both parties confirm the trade in TrustLayer.

A completed trade event is recorded.

Step 7 — Observation Window

After completion, the buyer may have a short observation period.

Example:

* 24 hours
* 3 days
* 7 days

During this time, the buyer may submit post-trade events if a clear issue appears.

⸻

4. Seller Journey

Step 1 — List Item

The seller lists an item on an existing marketplace.

Step 2 — Add TrustLayer Link

The seller may add a TrustLayer reputation link to the listing.

Example:

“Check my trading reputation on TrustLayer.”

Step 3 — Buyer Checks Reputation

A buyer uses the TrustLayer link to evaluate seller trustworthiness.

Step 4 — Trade Link Created

The seller or buyer creates a TrustLayer trade link for the specific item.

Step 5 — Trade Completed

Both parties meet and complete payment outside TrustLayer.

Step 6 — Reputation Updated

If both parties confirm completion, the trade becomes part of the seller’s and buyer’s reputation history.

⸻

5. TrustLayer Insertion Points

TrustLayer appears at three points.

Before Trade

Purpose:

Decision support.

User action:

Check reputation before contacting or buying.

During Trade

Purpose:

Record trade commitment and completion.

User action:

Join trade link and confirm completion.

After Trade

Purpose:

Record post-trade events.

User action:

Submit issue, evidence, response, or resolution.

⸻

6. Post-trade Observation

Some problems cannot be discovered during face-to-face inspection.

TrustLayer supports post-trade observation events.

Examples:

* Item works as expected
* Item does not match description
* Missing accessory
* Further communication required
* Evidence uploaded
* Seller response submitted
* Issue resolved
* Issue unresolved

These are recorded as events.

They are not automatic judgements.

⸻

7. What TrustLayer Does Not Do

TrustLayer does not:

* host the product listing
* process payment
* guarantee product quality
* decide who is telling the truth
* replace legal dispute resolution
* act as an escrow service

TrustLayer records behaviour and evidence.

Users interpret reputation.

⸻

8. Summary

TrustLayer supports the full trading lifecycle.

Before trade, it helps users evaluate trust.

During trade, it records confirmation.

After trade, it records follow-up events.

Trust is not built at one moment.

Trust is built across the lifecycle.
